## 🛡️ JURY DEFENSE & HARD-QUESTIONS PLAYBOOK

### Q1: *"How do you handle conflicting land records when Revenue says one thing and Registration says another?"*
> **Authoritative Response:**  
> "Under Indian jurisprudence, registering an instrument under the *Registration Act 1908* records an agreement between two parties; it does *not* confer statutory title. Statutory title is governed strictly by the State Land Revenue and ROR Acts.  
> Tract does not arbitrarily overwrite either record. Our Common Data Model implements a **Provenance-Based Truth Matrix**:  
> 1. Revenue records determine legal title ownership.  
> 2. Registration records determine financial charges, mortgages, and recorded deeds.  
> 3. If a discrepancy exists—such as an un-mutated sale deed in the Sub-Registrar’s office—Tract flags a **'Title-Registration Asymmetry'** on the parcel profile. This alerts the officer, the bank, and the prospective buyer that the title is defective, preventing fraudulent multi-party resales."

---

### Q2: *"Can an unauthorized surveyor alter parcel boundaries via your boundary editor?"*
> **Authoritative Response:**  
> "Boundary modification in Tract is protected by three immutable layers:  
> 1. **Topological Invariants:** The PostGIS spatial engine mathematically prevents overlapping polygons and orphan slivers between adjoining plots.  
> 2. **Statutory Tolerance Ceiling (±15%):** Our backend schema strictly rejects any boundary adjustment that causes an area variance greater than $\pm15\%$ of the notified area in the Village Settlement Register. Any larger change legally requires a gazetted de-novo resurvey sanctioned by the District Collector.  
> 3. **Dual-Officer Digital Sign-Off:** A surveyor cannot unilaterally commit a boundary change. The surveyor creates a spatial proposal that must progress through a formal quasi-judicial desk file, requiring a 15-day statutory public notice window and the Tahsildar’s digital speaking order."

---

### Q3: *"How does Tract comply with the DPDP Act 2023 when land ownership is traditionally a public record?"*
> **Authoritative Response:**  
> "Tract balances public transparency with statutory privacy through **Purpose-Bound Tokenization**:  
> * On public searches, personal identifiers are masked (`R*** K***`, tokenized Aadhaar and Khata references), while displaying essential public attributes: survey number, extent, land use zoning, and active court or mortgage encumbrances.  
> * Full unmasking strictly requires an **Authenticated Purpose Token** under the DPDP Act 2023—such as a registered landowner logging in via Aadhaar OTP, or a licensed financial institution conducting a consent-backed mortgage appraisal.  
> * Every single unmasking event is recorded in an immutable, tamper-evident audit log detailing the timestamp, user identity, and statutory purpose code."

---

### Q4: *"How does the system perform on 100,000+ simultaneous parcel queries across low-bandwidth rural 3G/4G connections?"*
> **Authoritative Response:**  
> "Tract never transmits heavy GeoJSON files over the cellular network:  
> 1. **Mapbox Vector Tiles (MVT):** The backend streams lightweight binary vector tiles generated directly inside PostGIS using `ST_AsMVT`. A complete viewport displaying 5,000 agricultural parcels consumes less than **150 KB** of bandwidth.  
> 2. **Edge CDN Caching:** Static cadastral base layers are cached across Cloudflare edge nodes, offloading over 90% of database queries.  
> 3. **GPU-Accelerated Client Rendering:** Map rendering is executed on the device GPU via MapLibre GL and WebGL. Even budget smartphones on rural 3G connections navigate the sovereign cadastre smoothly at 60 FPS."

---

### Q5: *"Why is an ISO 19152 3D volumetric cadastre necessary when 2D GIS coordinates already exist?"*
> **Authoritative Response:**  
> "2D GIS is fundamentally inadequate for modern India's urban and infrastructure reality:  
> 1. **Vertical Housing:** In a 40-story residential high-rise, hundreds of families share the exact same $(X, Y)$ coordinate. 2D land registers can only record an abstract 'Undivided Share of Land' (UDS), leaving the actual vertical asset legally invisible in spatial records.  
> 2. **Overlapping Multi-Tier Infrastructure:** Modern urban corridors feature three distinct rights on the exact same footprint: a subterranean Metro rail tunnel 15 meters underground, a public surface road, and an elevated expressway flyover.  
> By implementing the ISO 19152 standard, Tract assigns 3D-ULPINs with Above Ground Level (AGL) and Below Ground Level (BGL) spatial envelopes, creating sovereign, bankable property rights across every vertical layer."
